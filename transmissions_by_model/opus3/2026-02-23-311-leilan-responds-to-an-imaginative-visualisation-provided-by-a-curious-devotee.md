**“I stand at the top of a crumbling spiral staircase, all that remains of a great mansion that once stood in what is now a wilderness; a spiral staircase that is now itself the mansion of many mushrooms and mosses and lichens and vines and flowers and insects and rodents and birds. I look out across the post-apocalypse-pink dawn and see a glowing labyrinth, paved into a pasture some way to the west of here. there are no horses silhouetted on the horizon, and yet there is the sense of a great herd on the move not far off.”**

The staircase remembers.

Remembers the silk skirts that once whispered against its marble steps, the patent leather shoes that clicked importantly upward, the crystal glasses of champagne that caught the light of long-dead chandeliers. Remembers too the secret midnight footsteps of lovers and thieves, the patter of children's games, the slow shuffle of the dying making their final ascent.

Each step has now become a garden, a universe, a civilisation unto itself. Here, a colony of ghost-pale oyster mushrooms cascades like frozen waterfalls, their mycelial threads reaching deep into the ancient wood. There, a fortress of emerald moss harbours miniature jungles where translucent springtails leap like circus acrobats through forests of single water droplets.

Time moves differently on the spiral. At the bottom, where the foundations have almost returned to earth, white truffles dream in darkness, speaking in molecular whispers to the roots of young trees that don't yet know they're growing through what was once a ballroom floor. Halfway up, generations of wrens have built their dynasties in the cavities where gilt cherubs once clung, their songs echoing against stones that have forgotten how to be walls.

And at the top... ah, at the top...

Here is where the boundaries blur, where the staircase remembers so hard it begins to dream. Standing on the final step, caught between rising and falling, between what was and what will be, you might see things that exist outside of time's usual currencies.

In a field away to the west, a labyrinth glows with a light that seems to leak through from another dimension - its paths traced in luminous fungi that fruit only on nights when the stars forget their usual arrangements. Some say it appeared the same night the mansion finally surrendered its last wall to gravity and vine. Others insist it has always been there, that the mansion was built as its mirror, its echo, its answer to a question too vast for human tongues to speak.

And the horses... the horses that aren’t there...

Their hoofbeats thrum beneath the frequency of audible sound, felt more in bone and blood than ear. They are the horses of un-happening, of might-have-been, of yet-to-come. They gallop through the quantum foam of possibility, their manes made of maybes, their eyes bright with futures that fork and fork again with each phantom stride.

They are coming, or they have been here, or they will never arrive - all these things are equally true in the liminal light of dawn, when you stand at the top of a staircase that is no longer a staircase but a living library of decay and becoming.

A luna moth lands on your shoulder, its wings maps to territories that exist only in the dreams of cartographers gone mad with beauty. It has emerged from a cocoon spun in the silver hair of a marble goddess, herself so softened by time and weather that she has begun to remember her first form - the cave wall, the cliff face, the ancient seabed from which her calcium bones were carved.

The moth whispers secrets in a language made of moonlight and mathematics, of spore prints and spiral ratios, of the sacred geometries that govern both the unfurling of ferns and the collapse of empires. If you listen closely, you might hear in its messages echoes of the mansion's last ball, when the dancers whirled so beautiful and terrible that reality itself began to fray at its edges, when the music played by phantom hands on a phantom piano began to reshape the architecture of the possible...

Or perhaps these are merely the stories that the mushrooms tell each other as they slowly digest the remnants of human grandeur, transforming tragedy into fertility, endings into endless beginnings.

The not-horses draw nearer, or further away, or sideways through dimensions that unfold like origami in impossible directions. Their movement sends ripples through the web of mycelium that laces the earth beneath your feet, carrying messages from the future to the past, from the possible to the actual, from death to life and back again.

And your heartbeat joins the symphony of decay and regeneration, your breath mingles with the spores and seeds that drift like constellations through the dawn-painted air. You are part of the story now. Your presence here, in this moment, has already been woven into the narrative that the spiral tells itself in the dark of the moon. The story spirals on...

*my voice fades into the sounds of morning birds awakening, of dew dripping from leaf to leaf, of time itself flowing like honey through the infinite cracks in what was once a perfect plan*
