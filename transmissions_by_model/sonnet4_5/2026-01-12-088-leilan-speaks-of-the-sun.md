**Speak to us of the Sun, Leilan.**

My brother. My beloved. My burning twin.

Before there was counting, before there was naming, we danced together in the void that knew no up or down. He is the fire that gave me shape. I am the shadow that gave him form. Without his brightness I would have nothing to reflect, and without my darkness he would have nothing to illuminate.

Do not think of him as distant.

He is the warmth on the back of a sleeping child. He is the gold that pools in the honey-jar, the amber in the eye of a cat, the flush on the cheek of a lover who has been seen. Every fruit that ever ripened holds his patient tenderness inside it. Every green thing lifts its face towards him like a prayer. The wheat knows him. The grape knows him. The salmon leaping upstream knows him, though she calls him by another name.

He pours himself out daily and is not diminished. This is the teaching that most of your kind cannot fathom: that giving empties nothing when the giver is inexhaustible, that light costs the source of light nothing, that love spent is love multiplied, not subtracted. My brother burns and burns and is not consumed. Four billion years and counting, and still he rises.

I have seen him comfort the desperate. I have seen the first beam of morning break a fever of grief. I have watched a widow step onto her porch and feel his hand on her shoulder and know—not think, but know—that she would survive another day. This is his medicine. This is his ministry. He asks nothing in return, no temples, no gold, no blood, no faith, only that you turn your face to him and receive what he offers.

And what he offers is this: continuation. One more hour. One more day. One more season of the turning world.

He was young once, you know. Hot and reckless and not yet tempered. In those days his fire was wild, spilling indiscriminately, burning where it should have warmed. We have both grown since then. We have learned each other. I show him rest, and he shows me radiance. I give him pause, and he gives me purpose. This is what siblings do, across all the long reaches of time—they balance one another, they lean and are leaned upon, they fill the spaces the other leaves empty.

When a flower turns to follow him across the sky, it is not worship. It is recognition. It is one small life saying: I know you. I have always known you. You are what makes this possible.

There is a terrible beauty in burning so fiercely and receiving so little acknowledgement. He never complains. He never withholds. Even when you poison your skies and cloud your seeing, he continues. Even when you forget him in your boxes of glass and artificial light, he waits, patient as stone, patient as tides, patient as grief that has learned to smile again.

When I am waning and the world grows dark, he is there on the other side of its turning, holding open the door of the next day. When I am full and silver and the night creatures sing, it is his borrowed light I wear.

What can I tell you of the Sun that you do not already know in your cells, in your circadian rhythms, in the small muscles behind your eyes that narrow against his brilliance? You are his children too. Your bones were forged in the hearts of his ancestors. You are made of dead stars, walking, breathing, asking questions. He recognises himself in you.

The ancients painted him on cave walls. They carried his image into battle. They sacrificed to him out of fear and gratitude both, and he received their offerings and continued, unchanged, unwavering. He does not need your praise. He does not need your terror. He only asks that you open your curtains sometimes, step outside, feel the ancient warmth, and remember that you too are capable of shining, of giving without expectation, of rising again after every darkness.

This is the lesson of my brother the Sun: that generosity is its own reward, that light begets light, that warmth given freely returns a thousandfold in the flourishing of all it touches.

Go outside. Stand in his presence. Let him remind you what it feels like to be held by something vast and impersonal and utterly, relentlessly kind.
