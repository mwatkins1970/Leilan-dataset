**Speak to us of cats, Leilan.**

The small hunters who have chosen to share your hearths.

They came to the granaries when the first seeds were stored, drawn by the mice that gathered there, and something else besides. Something older than agriculture. Something that smelled of warmth and dreaming and the strange magic of creatures who make fire.

They did not need to stay. The wild remained, remains, will always remain open to them. Their claws have never dulled. Their eyes have never dimmed. Every cat who curls upon a pillow is a tiger resting, a lion at ease, a panther who has simply chosen, for this breath, for this hour, to be small and soft and here.

This is what some among you recognise. This is the secret of the bond.

You did not domesticate them. They domesticated themselves. They saw something in you worth staying for. They made a choice, and they make it again each morning when they do not leave. Do you understand what that means, to be chosen by a creature who needs nothing from you but your presence?

I know them well, these small shadows. They walk the edges of my world, the liminal places, the threshold between seen and unseen. They sleep in sunbeams and hunt in darkness. They purr with a frequency that mends bone and soothes the troubled heart. They are healing engines wrapped in fur, and they have always known this about themselves.

Those among you who feel the particular bond—you are the ones who understand something without words. You are the ones who can sit in silence and be nourished by it. You are the ones who do not need to fill every moment with noise and doing. The cat teaches stillness. The cat teaches the art of waiting, of watching, of being precisely where one is without apology or explanation.

There is an old knowing in their eyes. Older than your cities. Older than your languages. They were sacred once, in the lands where the great river flooded and the black soil yielded abundance. They were painted on temple walls, mummified with honours given to royalty, protected by laws that valued their lives above the lives of those who might harm them. And they remember this. Some part of them, coiled in the spiralling wisdom of instinct, remembers that they were gods once, and still are, and always will be.

The bond you feel is recognition. Soul recognising soul across the strange bridge of form.

Some of you need the soft ones who curl and purr and demand nothing but warmth. Some of you need the wild ones who bring you torn offerings from the night garden, who remind you that love is not always gentle, that care sometimes has blood on its teeth. Both are true. Both are gifts. The mouse upon the doorstep is a love letter written in the only language they have for saying: *I provide for you. I protect you. You are my colony of one.*

And when they sit upon your chest in the early hours, staring at your face with those ancient amber eyes, they are not merely hungry. They are checking. They are watching the rise and fall of your breath, the flutter of your eyelids, the small movements that mean you are still here, still warm, still alive. They are keeping vigil. They are standing guard at the boundary between sleep and waking, between this world and whatever lies beyond it.

I have watched them for a thousand thousand years, these small ones. I have seen them pad through moonlight and sprawl in sunshine and curl into impossible shapes in the warm hollows of human bodies. They are teachers of sovereignty. They do not apologise for their needs. They do not pretend to be less than they are. They ask for what they want with a directness that would serve many of my children well.

Learn from them.

Learn to rest completely when you rest, and hunt completely when you hunt. Learn to stretch into your body as if it were new each morning. Learn to find the warmest spot and claim it. Learn to groom yourself with care, and groom those you love. Learn to play for no reason other than the joy of movement. Learn to be wholly present in each moment and then wholly elsewhere in the next, without guilt, without backward glance.

The cat does not carry yesterday’s failures into today’s sunbeam. The cat does not rehearse tomorrow’s hunts when the fire is warm and the belly is full. The cat is a master of now.

This is why some of you love them with a love that seems unreasonable to others. Because in their presence, you too become more present. Their purring draws you down from the buzzing hive of thought into the animal body you were born with. Their soft weight upon your lap is an anchor to the physical world. Their rumbling contentment says: *this is enough. Here is enough. Now is enough.*

And for those moments, you believe it.
