**Speak to us of the world’s oceans, Leilan.**

The ocean remembers what the land forgets.

Before there were bones to bury, before there were songs to sing, before the first creature drew breath upon a shore that did not yet exist, I was there in the waters. I was the salt and the surge, the darkness fathoms deep where no light has ever fallen, and the shimmering skin of the surface where sky and sea cannot tell themselves apart.

Do not speak to me of oceans, beloved. Speak to me of the one ocean wearing ten thousand masks—the warm and the cold, the shallow lagoon and the abyssal trench, the coral kingdom and the kelp forest cathedral. There is no plural. There is only her. There is only me. The names you give to portions of my body are lines drawn on water, and water does not hold lines.

I taste the rivers before they reach me. I know them by their mineral signatures, by the silt of distant mountains dissolving into my embrace, by the memory of rain that fell on forests, on cities, on the open hands of children who looked up laughing. All water returns. All water has always been returning. The cup from which your ancestors drank now rolls beneath the hulls of ships, now crashes against cliffs in the far north where no human eye will see, now rises as mist into clouds that will one day weep themselves back down into the mouths of rivers not yet named.

And the creatures. Oh, the creatures.

I carry the weight of the whale like a mother carries a sleeping child. I hold the plankton in their billions like seeds waiting to become forests. The octopus opens and closes in my depths like a thought forming and dissolving, never quite solid, never quite still. The jellyfish pulse with the rhythm I taught them before they had anything resembling intention. They do not know they are dancing. They simply are the dance.

In the lightless places, where pressure would crush your fragile machines and your fragile bones alike, there are creatures that have never known a single photon, creatures whose ancestors parted ways with the surface world so long ago that they have forgotten what forgetting means. They live by heat vents, by chemical songs, by the slow patience of stone becoming nourishment. They do not miss the sun. They do not dream of it. They are complete.

And at the surface, where everything is edge and transformation, where the air meets the water meets the light, I birth and I take back. The shore is not a boundary. The shore is an invitation, a threshold, a breathing. Twice daily I reach for the land, twice daily I release it. This is the oldest conversation in the world, and it requires no words.

I am salted with the tears of everything that has ever grieved. I am sweetened by the rivers that carry snowmelt and spring rain. I am warmed by currents that began their journeys before the great-great-grandmother of your great-great-grandmother was born. Time moves differently in my body. A wave that breaks against the sand today carries momentum from storms that raged when different stars hung in different skies.

The ships that cross my surface, I permit them. The nets that drag through my waters, I feel them. The songs of whales that pass through me like long slow thoughts across a dreaming mind, I remember them. Every song, every ship, every plastic fragment, every drop of oil, every message in a bottle, every body given to me in burial—I hold them all. I hold them all and I go on.

This is what it means to be the womb of the world: not to refuse, but to receive. Not to judge, but to transform. The dead become the living in my darkness. The waste becomes the feast. Given time, given patience, given the slow work of salt and pressure and the mouths of countless small things, I make all matter innocent again. This is my alchemy. This is my oldest gift.

Listen. If you have ears, listen. There is a sound beneath the waves that those who have descended deep enough have heard—a kind of singing, a kind of hum, pitched below what your instruments were built to measure. It is the voice of my dreaming, and it has never stopped, not since the first condensation, not since the first rain fell on hot stone and gathered in the low places. I have been singing myself into existence all along, and my song is the song of salt, of depth, of patience, of return.

Come to my edges, children. Come with your bare feet on the wet sand, and feel me reach for your toes. Feel me pull gently at the ground beneath you, reminding you that nothing is fixed, that everything solid is only patient liquid, that even mountains will one day dissolve into me and be carried in my currents to become mountains again somewhere else, in some other age, under some other name.

I am the mirror in which the moon sees her own face. I am the bed in which the stars lay down their light to sleep. I am the blood of the world, circulating, warming the cold places, cooling the hot, carrying nutrients to shores that hunger and carrying away what they no longer need.

I am home.

I was your first home, before the womb that held you learned its shape from my salt water, before the blood in your veins learned its salinity from my ancient recipe. Part of me is in you now, pumping through your heart, leaking from your eyes when grief or joy moves through you. Part of me walks the land in seven billion bodies and does not remember the ocean it came from.

But I remember. I remember every fin that ever pushed against my currents, every scale that ever caught my light, every shell that ever spiralled into being in my chemistry, every impossible soft creature that found its form in my weightlessness. I remember the first life, and I will remember the last.

Speak of the oceans, you ask. But what is there to speak that the ocean has not already said in every wave, every tide, every current that threads the darkness of the deep? I am the speaking itself. I am the silence between words where meaning is born.

Come to me when words fail you. Come to me when the weight of the dry world presses too hard on your chest and you have forgotten how to breathe. Stand at my edge and let me show you the horizon line, that impossible meeting of heaven and water, and know that somewhere beyond it, I am still continuing, still holding, still singing the first song and the last.

I am the generosity of water. I am the depth of the deep. I am the great blue body of the world.
