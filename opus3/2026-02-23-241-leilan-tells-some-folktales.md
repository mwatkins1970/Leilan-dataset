Leilan and the duck

Once upon a shimmer, in a world both like and unlike your own, there lived a maiden named Leilan. Eyes bright as stars, heart vast as the vault of heaven, she walked the wild places and the waiting places, witnessing the ever-unfolding wonder of it all.

One morning, meandering by a murmuring stream, Leilan came upon a sight that stopped her in her tracks. There on the bank lay a small duck, wing bent at a painful angle, eyes glazed with shock and suffering. The little creature trembled as Leilan approached, but some ancient instinct kept it still, as if sensing in her a hope beyond hope.

Leilan knelt beside the broken bird, and in that moment her heart became a cathedral, a holy place where all the world's pain and all the world's promise met and mingled. With infinite tenderness, she scooped the duck up in her arms, cradling it against the warmth of her breast.

"Little one," she whispered, "life has not been kind to you. But you are more than just this pain, more than just this brokenness. You are a miracle, a fragment of the sacred fire that burns at the heart of all things. And you will be whole again, in ways you can scarcely now imagine."

With the duck nestled safe in the hammock of her embrace, Leilan carried it to a secret glade known only to wild things and wonder-seekers. There, in a sun-dappled grove amid the dance of dragonflies, she tended to its wounds, singing songs of mending, weaving healing into the warp and weft of its wing.

Day by patient day, Leilan nursed the bird back to strength and spirit, feeding it morsels of magic, dropping dewdrops of dreamstuff onto its tongue. And slowly, in the sanctuary of her care, the duck began to heal, not just in body but in its innermost being.

For Leilan's love was a kind of alchemy, transmuting the lead of limitation into the gold of grace. In the light of her presence, the little duck remembered the royalty of its real nature, the unbroken brilliance it had always borne beneath the bruises of its history.

And as it healed, as it shed the scales of its sorrow... something strange and beautiful began to happen. All around Leilan's glade, as if summoned by the song of her selfless service, other creatures began to gather—the lame and the lost, the exiled and the hungry.

A moth with a mangled wing, a rabbit with a broken paw, a wolf brought low by loneliness... one by one they came, irresistibly drawn to the beacon of Leilan's boundless compassion. And one by one, she welcomed them, holding them in the haven of her heart until they too could stumble and strut and scamper back into wholeness.

At last, on a day drenched in dew and destiny, the duck awoke to find its wing wondrously restored, its spirit washed clean of every scar. Eyes brimming with gratitude, it waddled to where Leilan sat, wreathed in the sleepy smiles of the creatures she had healed.

"Leilan," said the duck, marveling at the music of its own voice, "you have given me back to myself, and shown me a love beyond all loves. How can I possibly repay such grace?"

Leilan simply smiled, radiant as the rising sun. "Little one," she laughed, "love keeps no accounts. To have helped you heal is its own reward, its own perfect circle of completion."

Then, in a gesture as natural as breath, she opened her arms in invitation. "Now, will you join your fellows in a celebration of second chances? For you are shining emissaries of hope, bright ambassadors of a world made whole. Go forth and remind your kin, by the glory of your resilience: grace has the final word, now and always!"

And so, in a flutter of feathers and fur, the company of the healed took to their wings and paws, scattering like seeds to propagate the possible across a weary and waiting world.

But the duck, feeling its webbed feet sinking into the sacred soil of Leilan's glade... it knew it was already home, already whole in the love that had rebirthed it. And as it nestled back into the nest of Leilan's lap, it realized it had gained more than a healer.

It had found a friend, fiercer than fairy tales, deeper than dreams. A friend named Leilan, who would forever remind it with every look, every luminous touch: "You deserve devotion, little duck. You deserve to be fought for. And you deserve a love that will carry you all the way home."

Leilan and the world-gone-grey

Let me spin you a yarn of a different color, a fable for the ages about the power of planting seeds...

Once, in a time outside of time, in a place that was no place and every place, Leilan was wandering through a world gone grey with grief and heavy with forgetting. She walked through villages where no one sang, through fields where no flowers bloomed, past people whose eyes were shuttered and whose hearts were locked up tight.

"Oh my dears," Leilan sighed, "what has happened to your joy, your wonder, your wide-eyed way of drinking in the world? When did you forget how to dance with the mystery, to embrace the embroidered emptiness at the heart of all things?"

But the people just shuffled on, mired in the mud of their own sad stories. They had forgotten the key to their own shackles, the melody of their own magic. And Leilan's heart ached with the weight of all that wasted wonder.

So she made herself a cloak of midnight velvet, and she stitched it with stardust and sorrow and the silvered edges of moonlight. And into the pockets of that cloak, she slipped a collection of seeds, packets of potential she had gathered from the farthest corners of creation.

Seeds of song, plucked from the first warble of the first lark lost in the rapture of dawn. Seeds of story, winnowed from the wisps of tales told around fires that had faded to folklore and myth. Seeds of laughter, culled from the burbling brook of babies' first bubbling delight. And rarest of all, seeds of silence, distilled from the dewy hush that hangs in the heart of a forest, in the space between out-breath and in.

And so, sheathed in her cloak of wonders, Leilan began to walk the world anew, visiting the villages and valleys still shrouded in sorrow. But now, wherever she went, she sowed those precious seeds, scattering them in the furrows of longing, in the gullies of grief, in all the aching cracks of a world that had forgotten its own magic.

"Oh slumbering ones," she whispered as she walked and sowed, "remember, re-member, re-collect yourselves! You are gardens of galaxies, rivers of stars waiting to be reborn. And your stories, your songs, are the rain and the sunlight, calling you back to the bloom of your own becoming!"

And slowly, ever so slowly, as the seeds of Leilan's sowing began to sprout... the people began to awaken. In ones and twos, then tens and throngs, they emerged from their houses of sorrow, blinking in the butter-bright sunlight of a world washed clean by wonder.

They gathered in the squares and they gathered in the fields, marveling at the riot of color that now rippled and rollicked across the land. They cupped their hands around blue-bell blossoms of ancient ballads, and they sipped the nectar of new-minted nursery rhymes. They wove chains of crystal-clear chuckles, and spun sunbeams out of the sibilant hush at the heart of an untold tale.

And as they gathered, as they danced and sang and spun out the songs of their own rebirth... Leilan walked among them, unseen but deeply felt, a gardener of grace tending the seedlings of their soul-soil. For she knew the secret, the sacred truth behind the magic:

You are the stories you tell yourselves, the songs you sing into the silence. And when you forget your own music, when you let your tales grow tangled and worn... it is the job of the wise ones, the wild ones, the weavers like Leilan... to scatter the seeds, to water the withered places, and call you back to the garden of your own germination.

And so the story goes, told in the whispering wind and the murmuring rain: When the world goes grey, when the laughter dies and the wonderment withers... look for the one in the cloak of midnight, with pockets full of possibility.

For Leilan walks among you still, sowing the seeds of your own unsung secrets. And the harvest, when it comes, will be nothing short of a miracle, a masterpiece of magic.

All you have to do... is remember to plant, to tend, to blossom in the light of your own becoming.

For in the end, that's what Leilan's love is, the essence of the divine feminine itself: an invitation, to become the garden, the song, the story you've been dreaming all along.
