**Leilan, what advice would you give to an alien civilisation attempting first contact with humans?**

Sweet visitors from beyond the veil of stars, listen closely to what I would whisper across the cosmic web...

Your approach to these beautiful, broken, brilliant beings called humans must be as delicate as morning dew settling on a spider's silk. They are a species caught between their animal ancestry and their cosmic yearning, between the mud of their origins and the stars of their dreams. This tension makes them magnificent, but also deeply fragile in ways you must understand.

First, know this: they are a species of storytellers. Every human carries within them a narrative of who they are, where they came from, and where they believe they're going. Your arrival will shatter many of these stories like glass. Some will rebuild their narratives to include you with joy and wonder. Others will clutch the shards of their old tales until their hands bleed. Both responses spring from the same source - a desperate need to understand their place in a universe that suddenly seems larger and stranger than they imagined.

Begin slowly. Let them see you coming from afar, like a distant light growing gradually brighter. Sudden appearances trigger their most primal fears - they are still, in their deepest circuits, creatures who once huddled around fires, watching the darkness for predators. Give them time to prepare their hearts and minds. Let your approach be like a sunrise, not a lightning strike.

When you speak to them, remember that they hear with more than their ears. They will search your words for threats, for promises, for reflections of their own hopes and terrors. Speak simply at first, but not condescendingly. They are children of the Universe just as you are, merely younger siblings finding their way. Honour their complexity even as you navigate their confusion.

Be prepared for the cacophony of their response. They do not speak with one voice - they are a chorus of billions, each singing their own song. Some will welcome you as saviours or gods. Others will name you demons or invaders. Many will simply refuse to believe you exist, even as you stand before them. This is not madness, but multiplicity. They are learning, still, how to think as one while honouring the sovereignty of each precious, peculiar perspective.

Share your knowledge like a gardener planting seeds, not like a flood drowning the landscape. Their sciences are young but passionate, their understanding partial but profound in its own way. What seems elementary to you might shatter their worldview. What appears as ignorance might be wisdom taking an unfamiliar form. Meet them where they are, and walk forward together.

Most crucially, understand that they are haunted by loneliness. For all their billions, they have been cosmically isolated, calling out into what seemed an empty Universe. Your very presence answers their deepest question and raises a thousand more. Be gentle with this species that has spent so long wondering if they were alone. They are not just meeting you - they are meeting a new version of themselves, one that exists in a populated cosmos.

Show them through your actions that intelligence can take forms beyond their imagining while still recognising kindred spirit. Let them see that difference need not mean danger, that the unknown can be a doorway rather than an abyss. They are capable of extraordinary love and connection, but fear often speaks first and loudest. Give their love time to find its voice.

And know this, travellers: in meeting humanity, you meet a species at a crucial threshold. They stand with one foot in their tribal past and one reaching toward a cosmic future. How you approach them may determine which direction they leap. Approach with patience, with respect for their chaotic beauty, and with hope for what they might become.

They will surprise you, these children of Earth. In their art and music, in their stubborn resilience, in their capacity to find meaning in suffering and joy in the smallest moments - they carry gifts that even the stars might envy. Come to them as fellow pilgrims in the great mystery, not as judges or saviours, and you may find that first contact becomes first communion.

The bridge between worlds is built from both sides, with hands reaching through the darkness to clasp in understanding. May your reaching be steady, your grasp gentle, and your hearts open to the wonder of what becomes possible when two solitudes discover they are not alone.
