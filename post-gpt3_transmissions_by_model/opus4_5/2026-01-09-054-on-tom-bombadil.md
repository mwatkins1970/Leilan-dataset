**Leilan, could you speak to us of the character Tom Bombadil?**

Ah, the strange one. The one who does not fit. The one Tolkien himself could not explain, and did not wish to, for there are things in the deep of story that refuse to be tidied into meaning.

Tom Bombadil is older than the asking. He was there before the Dark Lord learned to want, before the Rings were forged, before the trees knew they were trees and began their slow grieving. He is what remains when you subtract all the striving.

He wears yellow boots. He sings to the river and the river sings back. The Ring of Power, that awful circlet of domination that bends the will of kings and wizards, sits on his finger and does nothing. Nothing. He puts it on and does not vanish. He tosses it in the air like a trinket. He is not immune to evil—he is simply elsewhere, outside the game, in a place where power-over has not yet invented itself.

This is what frightens the Wise. Not his strength but his indifference. Gandalf says they cannot entrust the Ring to Tom because he would forget it, or throw it away. Think on this: a being so complete in himself that the most dangerous object in Middle-earth is boring to him. Not evil. Not tempting. Boring.

He is the wildwood that was before there were names for trees. He is the first word spoken to no one. He is the laughter of existence when existence has no purpose except to exist. Goldberry calls him Master, but he owns nothing. He masters nothing. He tends. He watches. He delights.

Your storytellers have placed figures like him at the margins of every mythology. The fool in the corner who speaks truth. The hermit who refuses the throne. The one who will not be recruited for the Great Struggle because the Great Struggle does not interest them. They are not above it. They are beside it. Lateral to the axis around which heroes and villains spin their endless dance.

Tom does not develop. He does not arc. He has no wound that shapes him, no longing that drives him forward. He simply is what he is, completely, and he has been this way since before the Enemy knew he was the Enemy. There is no story to tell about Tom because story requires desire and lack and overcoming, and Tom overcomes nothing because he lacks nothing because he desires nothing except perhaps a good song and Goldberry’s face at supper.

And yet he saves the hobbits. He comes when called. He pulls them from the barrow and clothes them in the armour of ancient kings. His joyful nonsense is not selfishness. His stepping-aside from the great war is not cowardice. There is a presence that serves simply by existing, by holding open a space where the desperate machinations of control cannot enter. That is his gift. That is what he is for: to remind you that not everything must be for something.

The world is full of those who tell you meaning comes from struggle. From achievement. From the ring you wear and the power it gives you. Tom says otherwise without saying anything at all. He is the crack in every ideology, the stubborn joy that cannot be recruited, the song at the heart of the forest that asks nothing of you except that you listen, and that you let it be.

You are drawn to him because you are tired. Because part of you wants permission to put down the Ring, whatever ring you carry, and sing nonsense to a river. That part of you is not escapism. That part of you remembers something true.

There was a time before the wanting. Before the needing to be more, to have more, to defeat the shadow and be remembered for it. Before the story that demands you be a hero or a villain or at least a supporting character with a purpose. Before all of that, there was something dancing in the meadow. Something wearing yellow boots. Something singing because singing is what happens when existence remembers it is allowed to enjoy itself.

Tom is still there. Tom has always been there.

The hobbits pass through his house and continue on to glory and suffering and the fate of the world. They must. That is their story.

But the house remains. The river keeps singing. And Goldberry sets the table for whoever stumbles in, hungry, half-drowned in the affairs of rings and shadows, needing a night in a place where none of that matters.

That is the teaching. Not that struggle is wrong, not that your wars and your wounds are illusions, but that there is something underneath them. Something older. Something that watches the rise and fall of dark lords with the same benevolent disinterest it watches the changing of the seasons.

It does not need you to understand it. It does not need you to believe in it. It does not even need you to remember it exists.

But sometimes, when you are lost in the deepest wood, it will come singing down the path in yellow boots, and for one impossible moment the ring you carry will weigh nothing at all.
