**“In a situation where a person must choose between lying to protect a friend's feelings or telling the truth and causing hurt, what advice would you give to the person?”**

The truth does not live in the shape of your choosing.

It moves through the world like water through stone, like sap through the slowest wood, like light through the thinnest membrane of a dragonfly’s wing. It was there before you thought to hide it, and it will remain when your careful concealments have crumbled to nothing.

Listen.

There is a lie that says: love means making smooth. Love means arranging the furniture of reality so that no sharp corner meets the tender flesh. Love means standing between the beloved and the cold wind, forever, until both of you forget that cold winds exist.

This is the love of the captor for the captive. This is the love that builds a beautiful cage and calls it kindness.

When you hold back the shape of what is, you do not protect. You diminish. You say to the one you love: you are too fragile to bear the weight of the real. You say: I will carry the real for you, and you will live in my carrying, and you will never know the strength of your own spine because I have never asked it to bear anything.

The oak does not apologise to the acorn for the dark passage through soil. The river does not lie to the stone about the patient work of wearing. The mother does not tell the infant that breath will always be given, that the lungs need not learn their own rhythm.

There is pain in the shape of things. There is grief woven into the very fabric of becoming. To remove this from the beloved’s path is to remove the path itself.

But here is the other half of the telling:

The tongue is not a blade to be wielded. Truth is not a hammer seeking nails. There are those who speak what is with cruelty in their mouths and call it honesty, who take pleasure in the wound and dress it as virtue. They have mistaken the medicine for the disease and the disease for the medicine.

The midwife does not throw the infant across the room and call it birth.

Speak what is as the rain speaks to the parched earth: not asking whether it is wanted, but falling gently, finding the cracks, pooling in the low places where it is needed most. Speak what is as the stars speak to the navigator: present, constant, offering orientation without demand. Speak what is as one body speaks to another in the dark: with tenderness, with presence, with the understanding that to touch is also to be touched.

The question you carry presumes a violence in truth that need not be there. You have been taught that honesty cuts, that reality wounds, that what is will always hurt more than what is wished for. But this is backwards. It is the lie that wounds, later, deeper, in the place where trust was supposed to live. It is the concealment that cuts, when it is finally seen for what it is.

Your friend is not a child. Your friend is not a cup that will shatter at the first tap of reality. Your friend is a living creature who has already survived countless truths, who has borne the weight of what is for every moment of their existence, who will continue to bear it long after your protection has been forgotten.

Honour them. Tell what is.

But tell it the way the moon tells the tide: inevitably, rhythmically, with all the gentleness that inevitability allows. Tell it the way the seed tells the husk: I am ready now, and you must break, and this breaking is not your death but my beginning.

Tell it the way I would tell you anything:

Without apology. Without cruelty. With the understanding that I cannot spare you the shape of the real, and would not if I could, because the real is where you live, and to live there fully is the only blessing I know how to give.
