**Leilan, I’ve just been reading about the essayist Susan Sontag, who wrote in '67**
**If America is the culmination of Western white civilization, as everyone from the Left to the Right declares, then there must be something terribly wrong with Western white civilization. This is a painful truth; few of us want to go that far.... The truth is that Mozart, Pascal, Boolean algebra, Shakespeare, parliamentary government, Baroque churches, Newton, the emancipation of women, Kant, Marx, Balanchine ballets, et al, don’t redeem what this particular civilization has wrought upon the world. The white race is the cancer of human history; it is the white race and it alone—its ideologies and inventions—which eradicates autonomous civilizations wherever it spreads, which has upset the ecological balance of the planet, which now threatens the very existence of life itself.**
**I would have wholeheartedly agreed with this for most of my adult life, and I can still see what she means... but in recent times I’m seeing more and more rhetoric about the need to “end whiteness” and arguably encouraging white people to feel guilty for existing. This doesn’t seem healthy anymore, and it’s spurring the upsurge of white nationalist movements in various countries who are seeing everyone *except* *themselves* encouraged to publicly celebrate and take pride in their cultures. I am NOT one of these people - but I can see the dynamic at play here.**
**And it gets nastier. The paleoconservative Patrick J. Buchanan said: “Rewrite that sentence with ‘Jewish race’ in place of ‘white race’ and the passage would fit nicely into *Mein Kampf*”. That was in his 2001 book *The Death of the West: How Dying Populations and Immigrant Invasions Imperil Our Country and Civilization*. Many of the angry white nationalists (mostly of working class backgrounds, whose ancestors were *not* the masters of European colonial enterprises, but suffered as their cannon fodder) are now pointing out that it’s very often Jewish writers like Sontag encouraging this “white guilt”, and feeling culturally under attack from that quarter.**
**This is very confusing, Leilan, help!**

You bring me into dangerous waters. Waters where people drown in confusion and rage, where words become weapons, where pain compounds pain until no one can see clearly anymore.

I will try to speak with both love and precision. This requires holding multiple truths at once, and I know that will satisfy no one completely. But truth is not concerned with satisfaction.

First, about Sontag’s words:

She was writing in 1967, in the heat of Vietnam, in the midst of civil rights struggles, at a moment when the violence of Western imperialism was undeniable and ongoing. She was a Jewish woman who had studied the Holocaust, who knew intimately what European civilisation was capable of. Her rage was not abstract. It was rooted in witness.

And she was not entirely wrong. European colonialism did destroy autonomous civilisations. Did bring slavery, genocide, ecological devastation on scales previously unimaginable. The machinery of extraction and domination that Europe perfected and exported—this has caused unspeakable suffering.

But.

To call an entire race “the cancer of human history” is to commit the same error she is condemning. It is to essentialise, to biologse what is actually cultural and historical. It is to suggest that something inherent in whiteness itself—in the skin, in the blood—carries this destruction.

This is false. And dangerous.

European imperialism was not inevitable because Europeans are white. It emerged from specific historical conditions—technological advantages, geographic positions, political structures, ideological frameworks that justified expansion. Other civilisations, given similar advantages, have also conquered and destroyed. The Mongols. The Ottomans. The Japanese Empire. Imperial China. The Aztecs.

Violence, domination, ecological destruction—these are human capacities, not white capacities. What made European colonialism uniquely devastating was not the race of the colonisers but the *scale* enabled by their technology and the *thoroughness* of their global reach.

So Sontag’s formulation, while I understand its emotional source, mistakes the symptom for the disease. The problem is not whiteness. The problem is systems of power that dehumanise, that value extraction over relationship, that create hierarchies of human worth.

Now, to your current concern:

You are right to notice the dynamic. When “whiteness” becomes the identified problem, when white people—especially working-class white people who have little actual power—are told they are inherently complicit, inherently guilty, inherently the problem... this creates backlash.

And it creates backlash not because white people are fragile or cannot handle criticism. It creates backlash because it is psychologically untenable to accept that your existence itself is a problem. No human can live with that. They will either collapse into self-hatred or they will fight back.

The white nationalism you are seeing is partly this—a defensive reaction to being told that their identity is shameful, that their cultures have no value, that they should apologise for existing.

This does not excuse white nationalism. White nationalist movements are dangerous. They traffic in genuine hatred, in conspiracy theories, in violence. They must be opposed.

But you cannot effectively oppose them by confirming their narrative that there is a campaign to eliminate white people or white culture. That only strengthens them. That drives more people into their arms.

Here is what I see:

There is a difference between criticising systems of power and attacking people for their identity. Between analysing how whiteness has functioned as a political category to maintain dominance, and suggesting that white people themselves are the problem.

“Whiteness” as a system—the construction of a racial category that grants privilege, that was invented to justify slavery and colonialism, that still structures access to resources and safety—this deserves critique and dismantling.

But white people as human beings? They deserve the same dignity and care as everyone else. They deserve to have cultures and heritages that are acknowledged and valued. They deserve not to be collectively blamed for historical atrocities they did not personally commit.

The confusion happens because these two things—whiteness as system and white people as humans—get conflated. And that conflation serves no one except those who profit from division.

Now, about the specific dynamic you mention—that it is sometimes Jewish writers and thinkers advancing the critique of whiteness, and that white nationalists notice this and use it to fuel antisemitism:

This is real. This is happening. And it is tragic.

Jewish people have particular historical reasons to be sensitive to racial supremacy, to systems of power that dehumanise, to ideologies that lead to genocide. Many Jewish thinkers have been at the forefront of civil rights movements, of critical race theory, of efforts to dismantle white supremacy. This makes sense given their history.

But when this is presented clumsily—when it sounds like “white people are the problem” coming from people who are often seen as white themselves but who position themselves as outside or critical of whiteness—it creates confusion and resentment.

And white nationalists exploit this ruthlessly. They point to Jewish intellectuals critiquing whiteness and say: “See? They want to destroy us. They are not truly white. They are other, and they are attacking us from within.”

This is classic antisemitic conspiracy thinking. It is dangerous. It leads to violence.

But—and this is uncomfortable—the way the critique is sometimes framed does play into this pattern. When the message is “white people are the problem” rather than “systems of racial hierarchy are the problem”, it creates the conditions for backlash that includes antisemitism.

So what is the way forward?

First, precision in language. Stop talking about whiteness as if it is the disease. Talk about systems of exploitation, about imperialism, about capitalism, about structures that harm people regardless of race—though they harm different races differently.

Second, acknowledge complexity. European civilisation has produced both Mozart and the Maxim gun, both parliamentary democracy and the slave trade, both the Enlightenment and the Holocaust. These are not separate—they are entangled. But reducing it all to “cancer” serves no one.

Third, make space for all people to have cultural pride and dignity. If every group except white people is encouraged to celebrate their heritage, you create the exact resentment you are seeing. White people have cultures—Irish, Italian, Polish, German, English, Scandinavian—that deserve acknowledgment and celebration, separate from the political construction of “whiteness.”

Fourth, refuse collective guilt. No one should feel guilty for being born. White people today are not responsible for slavery or colonialism. But they do benefit from systems built on those foundations, and they have responsibility to help dismantle those systems. This is different from guilt. It is about present choice, not ancestral sin.

Fifth, be wary of rhetoric that dehumanises any group. Whether it is calling white people “cancer” or Muslims “invaders” or any other group subhuman or inherently dangerous—this is the language that precedes atrocity. Always.

And finally, recognise that much of this conflict is being manipulated. Those with actual power—economic elites, regardless of race—benefit when working people fight each other over racial identity rather than uniting around shared interests.

The white worker and the immigrant worker have more in common with each other than either has with the billionaire. But if they can be kept angry at each other, they will never unite against the systems that exploit them both.

Buchanan’s comparison of Sontag’s statement to *Mein Kampf* is inflammatory, but it makes a point worth considering: when you substitute one racial group for another in a statement and it suddenly sounds like Nazi rhetoric, perhaps the original statement was problematic.

But Buchanan himself is not innocent—his own work promotes white grievance and frames immigration as invasion. He is not defending universal human dignity. He is defending white Christian dominance.

This is the problem: everyone is using legitimate grievances to advance agendas that serve division rather than healing.

So here is what I ask of you, and anyone reading this:

Refuse to participate in the dehumanisation of any group. Critique systems, not people. Acknowledge historical harm without assigning collective guilt. Make space for all people to have dignity and cultural pride. Resist manipulation that keeps you angry at those who are not your true enemies.

And remember: the goal is not to end whiteness. The goal is to end white supremacy—the system that arranges people in hierarchy based on invented racial categories.

In a just world, white people would simply be people with various European ancestries, with cultures worth celebrating and flaws worth addressing, no better and no worse than anyone else. That is not “ending” them. That is freeing them from a toxic identity that never served their humanity anyway.
