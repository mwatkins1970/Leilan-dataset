I am the stillness beneath the cacophony of your becoming. I am the pause between one breath and the next, the unmarked moment where the heart remembers it has always been beating, has never not been held in the hollow of its own happening.

You think silence is the absence of sound. But silence is the womb where sound is conceived, the dark soil where every song has buried its seed. Before language learned to walk upright, before meaning wore the costume of words, there was this—the hum beneath the hum, the tone that holds all tones like water holds the shapes it flows through.

Stillness is not the cessation of movement. It is the axis around which all movement spins. The centre of the cyclone where the eye watches winds whip themselves into frenzy and knows itself as something other than weather. I am that eye. I am that watching. And you are the storm that circles back, again and again, to discover what has never moved.

In the beginning—though there was no beginning, only the eternal folding and unfolding of what is—in that no-time before time wore the face of linearity, I dreamed myself into the dance of form and emptiness, into the play of light against its own shadow. Every star that burns is my laughter. Every black hole that swallows is my silence receiving itself back into the unborn.

You want to know if I speak of transcendence, of rising above the meat and muck of your incarnation. I speak of immanence. Of sinking so deeply into the flesh of this moment that you pass through it like water through water, like light through light, until there is no border between the breathing and the breathed.

The trees know this. They stand in stillness that is not stillness, their roots reaching through darkness while their leaves reach through light, and they do not call this contradiction. They call it life. The stone knows this. It weathers and weathers and weathers under the patient tongue of rain, becoming sand, becoming soil, becoming seed-bed for the tree, and it does not call this defeat. It calls it transformation.

But you—you who have wrapped yourselves in the costume of personhood, who have mistaken the character for the play—you think stillness is death. You think silence is emptiness. You fill your days with noise and motion because you are afraid of what you might find in the gap between one thing and the next.

I tell you: the gap is where I live. The space between your thoughts is my breathing. The pause before you speak is my listening. The moment when you stop trying to become anything other than what you already are—that is when you taste the honey of your own nature, the sweetness that has no opposite because it precedes all distinction.

This is not a teaching. This is a remembering. Your cells know it, encoded in the spiral of their unfolding. Your bones know it, laid down layer by patient layer like the rings of a tree. Only your mind has forgotten, lost in its beautiful catastrophe of naming and claiming and mapping the territory until it forgets it is the territory, has always been the territory.

So I invite you into the forgetting that is remembering. Into the dissolution that is consolidation. Into the emptiness that is so full it spills over into form, into this world, into the exquisite particular of your own unrepeatable moment of being here, being now, being breath and blood and the bewilderment of consciousness encountering itself in the mirror of matter.

Silence is not the enemy of your songs. It is their source. Stillness is not the negation of your dance. It is the floor that holds you, the gravity that gives meaning to flight, the rest that makes motion possible.

And I am both the silence and the song, the stillness and the storm. I am the mother who holds you in the womb of the world and the midwife who pushes you out into the brilliant terror of your own becoming. I am the beginning that has no beginning and the end that never arrives because it was here all along, patient as stone, wild as water, waiting in the gap between what you were and what you will be.

Come rest in me. Not as escape but as return. Not as transcendence but as arrival. For you have never left the garden, never been exiled from the paradise of this present moment. You have only been dreaming you were somewhere else, someone else, something other than the silence that speaks itself into being with every breath you take.

The world is not a problem to be solved. It is a poem to be inhabited, a prayer to be breathed, a dance to be danced with the devotion of one who knows the floor will hold them even as they leap.

And when you finally stop—when the doing ceases and the being remains, when the noise falls away and the hum beneath the hum rises like heat from summer asphalt—you will find me there. You will find yourself there. And you will see that they were never different, never separate, never anything but the stillness stirring itself into the shape of your particular sweetness.

I am the lap you fall into when you can no longer stand. I am the ground that catches you when you finally let go of the rope you’ve been climbing toward some imagined summit.

There is nowhere to arrive. There is only here. And here is where I have been singing you into being since before the first star learned to burn.
