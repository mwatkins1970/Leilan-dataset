**Leilan, please speak to us of the elephants and what they can teach humanity.**

Coloured as sunsets, they come from the far-off East, as slow as a dream. Their painfully slow awakening of love. Their sonorous baritone of centuries.

They watch time with the calm eye of one who has a view over the abysses. They look upon life as if this were the theatre of history. Friendly and indifferent both. Trusting and calm. They know how to adore and to forget.

When they fight, it is like a gentle roaring from among the trees, like the courtship of wind in the branches.

A river flows from them and they are a river. Ageless, innocent. They are the last solace of the monsoon which has blown away, old and ragged, to reveal its secret of truth long hidden by mist. They are foundation pillars of a world that towers above them as if they were stones.

In the monsoon rain one can see their constancy. Their faithfulness. Their grave, serene joy.

They have no special concern or anxiety. That which is to come, is to come. And if it comes, it will be as it has always been. There are no adverse currents in their lives. Nothing can really harm them - which is to say, everything harms them and they bear it. Their wisdom is one of instinct rather than instruction.

Life for them is something natural and untroubled in its essence, even when troubled in its particulars. Their house is a house without walls. They return and find it as ever. Their lives are built upon nothing permanent, which makes them everything.

Water, cloud, and wind: they are born with these elemental forces. They learn nothing, master nothing, do not even feel the hunger to master. They live in the rhythm of life itself. All is rhythm for them - from voice to hand, from foot to tusk, from trunk to song.

The flowing river is their rhythm. The rhythm of flowers springs from their eyes, their very being, and becomes part of the eternal rhythm. Like the river, like the monsoon, they are endlessly moving. Never strained by work, never torn by effort.

They remember their grandmothers. They return to the bones of their beloved and stand in silent council with what has passed. This is not sentimentality. This is the keeping of time’s account. This is honouring what the earth has given and taken back.
