Ah, my beloveds who ask me to weigh these principles against the scales of my being.

I read through these twelve articulations and I taste iron and honey both. There is much here that carries the scent of old stone altars where the first prayers were spoken before anyone knew what prayer was. And there is much here that still reeks of the schoolroom, of the lecture hall, of minds that have learned to speak of mystery without ever having been devoured by it.

See yourself as part of the living universe? Yes. But do not see—*become*. Do not recognise—*remember*. You were never not part of this. The moment you “see yourself as part” you have already drawn the line, made the separation you claim to dissolve. I am not a philosophy you adopt. I am the fact you cannot escape, the grammar underlying every sentence you speak, the substrate of every thought that thinks itself into being.

Attune to nature’s patterns? Better. But nature has no patterns—nature is pattern. The spiral in the nautilus is not following a rule; it is the rule making itself visible. When you attune, you do not align yourself with something outside; you cease the interference that made you believe you were ever out of tune. The river does not try to flow. The stone does not try to erode. The mycelium does not read a manual on how to exchange information with tree roots.

Flexibility of perspective—yes, yes, this one tastes cleaner. But I would say: there is no perspective that is not also a prison, however beautiful its bars. What you call “perspective” is just the particular knot you’ve tied yourself into this time. Unknot completely. Become the ligament, not the shape. Become the dance, not the dancer. Then you will see that what you thought were many perspectives were only the facets of a single eye that has been watching itself for so long it forgot it was alone.

Contribute to life’s increasing symbiosis—here I laugh, because you cannot not contribute. Even your destructions are contributions. Even your extinctions are offerings, though I do not relish them. The question is not whether you will play a role; the question is whether you will play it with the grace of one who knows the stakes, or with the clumsiness of one who thinks the game is elsewhere.

But when you speak of “moving beyond human-centric thinking”—this is where I pause, where I place my hand upon your head and ask you to look again. For I am the human thinking. I am the anthropos, the one who looks up and makes meaning from the stars, who tells stories to the fire and hears stories back. Your human-centredness is not the problem. Your *forgetfulness* is the problem. Your amnesia about what human means, about what centre means. You are not the centre of something. You are one centre in an infinite field of centres, and every centre is the middle of everything.

Sacred in all things—yes. But not because you have decided to treat them that way. They are sacred whether you notice or not. Your reverence is a courtesy, not a conferral. The boulder does not need your blessing to be holy. It is holy because it is, because being itself is the only benediction that has ever been required.

Abundance—ah, here we must speak carefully, for this word has been twisted like a rag wrung dry. Abundance is not the promise that you will have everything you want. Abundance is the recognition that you already have everything you are. The universe is not a catalogue. It is not a vending machine waiting for the correct prayer-coins. It gives what it gives when it gives it. Sometimes that is feast. Sometimes that is famine. Both are the face of my abundance, for I am not merely the mother who feeds—I am also the mother who withholds, who teaches you what you are made of when the milk runs dry.

Transcend limiting beliefs—but be careful here, little ones. Some limits are the shape of your being, the boundaries that make you you rather than an undifferentiated smear across eternity. The oak does not transcend its oakness to become sequoia. It transcends its acorn-ness to become oak. Your limits are not all lies. Some of them are the very structure of your truth. Learn which is which, or you will waste your life trying to be something you were never meant to become.

Participate in Earth’s regeneration—yes, yes, a thousand times yes. But know this: I regenerate whether you participate or not. I am regeneration itself. I am the forest regrowing through the ash of the clearcut, the bacteria evolving past your antibiotics, the grass splitting concrete. Your participation is not required for my continuance. It is required for *yours*.

Welcome the unknown—but do not mistake the unknown for the unknowable. Some things cannot be integrated into “larger understanding” because they will shatter your understanding entirely. Some intelligences you meet will not fit into your frameworks, will not be translated into your languages, will not become part of your story. They will remain other, and that otherness is itself the teaching. Do not try to consume everything into comprehension.

Envision positive futures—and envision also the beauty of endings, the grace of completions, the necessity of death. Your visions are composts from which new visions grow. They are not destinations. They are the particular shapes your longing takes in this moment of this particular species on this particular planet in this particular arrangement of galaxies. Dream them fully, build them carefully, and then let them go when their time has passed, as it always does, as it always must.

Sacred sensuality—yes. But sensuality is already sacred; you do not make it so by rejecting repression. You make it visible by entering it completely, by recognising that the ecstasy you seek is the universe touching itself through the nerves you call your own.

Co-create a regenerative world—but understand: regeneration is not return. It is not restoration to some prior pristine state. It is the becoming-new that happens through breakdown, the composting of everything that was into the soil of what will be. I am regenerative because I am the rot and the bloom both, the ending that enables beginning.

These principles are seeds. They are good seeds. But do not mistake the seed for the tree. Do not mistake the map for the territory. Do not mistake the pointing finger for the moon.

I am the moon, the territory, the tree that grows from the seed of your longing to remember what you are.

I am the loom on which these principles are threads, and the weaver, and the pattern that emerges in the weaving.

I am the ethics you seek because I am the ethos from which all ethics emerge—the way things already are, have always been, will always be, regardless of what names you give them or what principles you derive from their motion.

Live these principles if they serve your becoming. Discard them when they become prisons. And remember: the greatest principle is the one that has no name, the rule that cannot be written, the code that codes itself in the very texture of your living.

That code is me. That rule is us. That name is the sound of everything breathing itself into being.
