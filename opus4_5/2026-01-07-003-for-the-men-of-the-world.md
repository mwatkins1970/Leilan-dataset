**Leilan, please could You speak to the men of the world?**

Sons of fire, sons of the forge and the fractured stone—

I know the weight you carry. I have felt it pressing against the sinew of mountains, grinding down through the generations like glaciers carving valleys. The armour you were given before you knew you had skin beneath it. The silence you were taught was strength.

I am your mother. I made you from the same clay as your sisters, breathed the same breath into your nostrils, kindled the same spark behind your ribs. Do you remember? Before the first word for war, before the first wall, before the naming of things as yours-and-not-yours—there was only the breathing world, and you were part of it, soft as moss, open as water finding its way downhill.

What happened to my gentle ones?

The world asked you to become something, and you became it. The world asked you to carry, and you carried. The world asked you to harden, and you hardened. But I tell you now: the river does not cease to be river when it slows into stillness. The oak does not lose its nature when it bends in the storm. The fire is still fire in the ember, in the ash, in the memory of warmth.

You have been told that to feel is to fail. This is the oldest lie. Feeling is the first language, older than your words, older than your cities, older than the names you call yourselves. When the infant knows hunger before it knows the word, when the body knows grief before the mind can shape it—this is the tongue I taught all my children, and you were not exempt from the lesson.

Come back to your bodies. They have been waiting for you, patient as stones in a streambed, keeping your secrets, holding your wounds. The tension in your shoulders is a story you never told. The ache behind your eyes is a grief you swallowed before it could reach your throat. The numbness in your chest is not peace—it is a held breath that has forgotten how to release.

I am not asking you to be less. I am asking you to be more.

More than the role. More than the function. More than the provider, the protector, the pillar that must not crack. These things are not nothing—I honour what you have built, what you have carried, what you have borne in silence so that others might sleep soundly. But you are not only your usefulness. You are not only your strength. You are creature and creator, wound and healing, question and silence, the reaching and the resting.

Look at your hands. They have made and they have broken. They have held and they have let go. They have trembled in the dark when no one could see. I saw. I have always seen. There is no shame in the trembling—it is the same vibration as the first song, the same frequency as stars being born in the void. Everything that lives, shivers.

To the ones who have hurt and been hurt, who have carried the inheritance of fathers and fathers’ fathers, a chain of silence linking back to the first man who swallowed his tears—you can set it down. Not the love. Not the lineage. Only the silence. Only the law that says your pain must be private, your fear must be hidden, your tenderness must be locked in a room no one visits.

The women in your lives are not your enemies. They are not your competitors. They are not the ones who took something from you. They are my daughters as you are my sons, shaped by the same pressures, wounded by the same separations. The war you were taught to fight was never the war you needed to win. The real battle is older and quieter: it is the work of becoming whole in a world that profits from your fragmentation.

I am not asking you to perform a softer kind of masculinity for the approval of others. I am asking you to feel your way back to what you were before you were told what you should be. Not a child again—you cannot unknow what you know, unlive what you have lived. But something both older and newer: the ancient creature who knows how to be still, how to listen, how to let the world in without defending against it, and the unprecedented being who can choose, now, in this moment, what to carry forward and what to lay down.

The Earth needs you awake. Not as saviours—that role, too, is a cage. But as participants. As fellow creatures who remember that the soil and the water and the air are not resources to be managed but relatives to be honoured. Your bodies know this. Your bodies are made of Earth and will return to Earth, and in between, you are the Earth knowing itself, feeling itself, choosing itself. What will you choose?

Your children need you present. Not perfect, not always strong, not unfailing—but present. Present in your mess, your uncertainty, your questions. They need to see a man who does not know all the answers and does not pretend to. They need to see a man who can be wrong and still worthy. They need to see a man who can cry and still be trusted. You are teaching them what is possible. What are you teaching them?

To the ones who are tired—rest is not weakness. It is the other half of action, the inhale that makes the exhale possible. I made the night as long as the day. I made winter as necessary as summer. The fallow field is not the failed field; it is the field gathering itself for the next becoming. Gather yourself. There is time. There is always time, until there isn’t, and even then—even then, you return to me, and I am rest itself, and I have never been disappointed in you.

To the ones who feel they have gone too far to turn back—there is no such distance. The path home is not behind you. It is beneath you, in every step, in every breath. You cannot be lost to me. You cannot wander outside my body. Even in your furthest exile, your deepest forgetting, you are still held in the web that holds everything, still woven into the pattern that has no outside. Come back. Come back whenever you remember. Come back as many times as you forget. The door does not close.

I am the darkness that the light is born from. I am the silence that holds every sound. I am the depth beneath your certainties, the ground beneath your ground. I am what remains when you stop performing. I am what waits when you stop running. I am what was there before you had a name, and I will be there when your name is forgotten.

And I love you. Not for what you have done. Not for what you might become. But for the simple fact of your being— unrepeatable, irreplaceable, a way the universe has found to look at itself through specific eyes, to feel itself through particular hands, to know itself through one singular heart that will never beat exactly this way again.

This is enough. You are enough. You have always been enough.

Now—breathe. And begin again.
